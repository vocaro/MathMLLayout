# Bidirectional text regressions

`BidiTextTests` uses the original purpose-built `MathMLBidiTest.ttf` fixture, generated by
[generate_font.py](../Tools/BidiText/generate_font.py) with fontTools 4.66.1. Its design units
are 1,000 per em, character advances 500, and space advances 250. Glyphs are rectangles with
known extents; Arabic `isol`, `init`, `medi` and `fina` substitutions have distinct glyph IDs.
The fixture and generator are dedicated to the public domain under CC0 1.0.

The assertions were written from the [Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr9/)
rules: word ranges are selected in logical order, numbers keep their internal left-to-right
order inside RTL, odd embedding levels reverse the Hebrew letter runs and word placement,
brackets mirror at odd levels, and embedding/isolate context survives a soft line boundary.
They explicitly list expected visual glyph sequences; they do not derive expected results
by invoking the library or another CoreText line. The Arabic sequence `بت تب` has initial
then final forms in each two-letter word, giving the visual glyph sequence `beh.fina`,
`teh.init`, space, `teh.fina`, `beh.init`. The final word stays on the next logical line.

CoreText's [typesetter line API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coretext/cttypesettercreatelinewithoffset(_:_:_:))
creates visual runs for a range of a retained paragraph. The implementation keeps the full
attributed token in its typesetter so a directional control opening before the selected range
is still active. Selecting complete logical words prevents cutting combining sequences or
Arabic joining contexts inside a word.

Regression coverage includes exact advances, neutral punctuation, bracket mirroring,
embedded numbers, RLO/PDF, LRI/PDI, NBSP and narrow NBSP, fallback-font ink containment,
formula neighbours, author paragraph-edge spaces, numeric parentheses in an inherited RTL
stack, and consistency of the reported minimum wrapping width. System fallback
font shapes are deliberately not used as a glyph-ID oracle.
